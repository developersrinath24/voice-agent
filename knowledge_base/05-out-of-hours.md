# Out of hours and emergencies

## When the surgery is closed

The surgery is closed on weekday evenings after 18:30, all day Sunday, on
Saturday afternoons, and on bank holidays. During these times:

- For urgent medical advice that cannot wait, call **NHS 111** free of charge, any
  time of day or night.
- For a life-threatening emergency, call **999** immediately.

The surgery telephone line plays a recorded message outside opening hours and
redirects urgent calls to NHS 111. The practice does not provide an out-of-hours
service itself.

## Call 999 immediately for

- chest pain or pressure that does not go away
- difficulty breathing or choking
- signs of a stroke, such as a face drooping, arm weakness or slurred speech
- severe bleeding that will not stop
- loss of consciousness, fits or a seizure that does not stop
- a severe allergic reaction with swelling of the face or throat
- thoughts of harming yourself or someone else

## Call NHS 111 for

- an urgent problem that is not life-threatening
- advice about whether you need to be seen
- a problem that starts when the surgery is closed
- concerns about a child who is unwell

## Urgent treatment centre

Kingsmere General Hospital has an urgent treatment centre open from 08:00 to
22:00 every day for minor injuries, minor burns, sprains, and suspected
fractures. It is not an emergency department. The nearest emergency department
is at Ashford Vale Hospital, which is open 24 hours.

## Accidents and injuries

Patients who have had an accident, a fall, a burn or a suspected fracture should
go to the urgent treatment centre or the emergency department rather than waiting
for a GP appointment. The surgery cannot X-ray or stitch serious wounds.

## Mental health crisis

Anyone in a mental health crisis can call NHS 111 and select the mental health
option, contact their GP during opening hours, or call 999 if there is an
immediate risk to life. The practice also has a mental health practitioner
available two afternoons a week by appointment.

## Dental problems

The surgery cannot treat dental pain or dental infection. Patients should contact
their dentist, or call NHS 111 if they do not have one. Antibiotics are not
prescribed by the practice for dental problems.
