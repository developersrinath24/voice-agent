# Practice policies

## Privacy and your data

The practice holds medical records in line with UK data protection law. Records
are shared with other NHS services involved in a patient's care, and with the
NHS national data systems where the law allows.

Patients can:

- ask for a copy of their record, free of charge, with a response within one month
- ask for incorrect information to be corrected
- register an objection to sharing their data with NHS research programmes

## Chaperones

Any patient can ask for a chaperone during an examination. This is free, does not
need a reason, and should be requested when booking so the appointment is
arranged with a member of staff available to chaperone.

## Zero tolerance

The practice expects patients and staff to treat each other with respect. Verbal
abuse, threats, discrimination and physical aggression are not acceptable. The
practice may remove a patient from the list in serious cases, and the patient is
told in writing how to appeal.

## Complaints

The practice takes complaints seriously and treats them as an opportunity to
improve.

1. Raise the concern with reception or the practice manager. Many issues are
   resolved immediately.
2. If not resolved, make a formal complaint in writing to the practice manager.
   The practice acknowledges within three working days.
3. A full written response follows within 28 working days. If more time is needed,
   the practice explains why and gives a new date.

Complaints should normally be made within 12 months of the event. A complaint can
also be made to NHS England or the local Healthwatch, and patients can ask their
MP to refer the complaint to the Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman.

## Feedback

Feedback, both positive and negative, can be left in the suggestion box at
reception, by email, or through the friends and family test link sent by text
message after some appointments.

## Infection control

The practice follows national infection prevention guidance. Hand gel is
available at the entrance and in every consulting room. The treatment room is
deep cleaned daily.

## Safeguarding

All staff have a duty to protect children and vulnerable adults. Concerns are
shared with the local safeguarding team. The practice will not always be able to
discuss a safeguarding referral with the patient, but will explain as much as the
law allows.

## Care for people with additional needs

Reception can record communication preferences, such as texting instead of
calling, or needing a longer appointment because of a learning disability or
dementia. Annual health checks are offered to patients on the learning disability
register.
