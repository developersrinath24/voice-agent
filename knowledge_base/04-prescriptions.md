# Repeat prescriptions

## How to order

Repeat prescriptions can be ordered in three ways:

1. Through the NHS App or the online service from the practice website.
2. By handing a repeat request slip to any pharmacy in Kingsmere that offers the
   electronic prescription service.
3. By leaving a written request in the prescription box at reception.

The practice does not take repeat prescription requests over the telephone,
because of the risk of error.

## How long it takes

Repeat prescriptions are processed within **two working days**. Requests are not
actioned on the same day. Patients should order when they have about seven days
of medicine left.

Requests for medicines that are not on a patient's repeat list, or that have
been stopped, need a GP review and can take up to five working days.

## Controlled drugs

Medicines classed as controlled drugs, including strong painkillers and some
sleeping tablets, need five working days and may require a review appointment.
They are not issued early, and lost or stolen prescriptions are not replaced
without a GP consultation.

## Repeat prescriptions reviews

Most repeat medicines are authorised for 12 months. When a review is due, the
prescription shows "review due" and a review appointment is needed before
further supplies are issued. The practice will send a text message when a review
is approaching.

## Where to collect

Prescriptions are sent electronically to the pharmacy the patient has nominated.
No paper prescription is printed unless the patient is not registered with a
pharmacy. Patients can change their nominated pharmacy by telling any pharmacy
or by updating the NHS App.

## Prescription charges and exemptions

Standard NHS prescription charges apply. Patients who are exempt, including
those with a valid medical exemption certificate or a prepayment certificate,
should make sure their exemption is recorded. The practice cannot refund a charge
that has already been paid at a pharmacy.

## Medicines you can buy without a prescription

The practice follows NHS guidance and does not routinely prescribe items that can
be bought over the counter, such as paracetamol, antihistamines, sun cream and
vitamins, unless there is a clinical reason.
